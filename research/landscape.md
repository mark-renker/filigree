# Landscape: text/code music composition environments, harmony tools, and expressive MIDI
### Quality bar and gap analysis for a new text language focused on per-note EXPRESSION + complex HARMONY

Scope: ~35 systems across five families — live-coding languages, notation/score languages, harmony/generative plugins, expressive MIDI/MPE tooling, and text formats that already encode continuous expression. Every claim below is sourced from primary docs where possible. Evaluation axes: **(a)** continuous per-note expression as text, **(b)** harmony/voice-leading concision, **(c)** ideation speed, **(d)** DAW/MPE output.

---

## 1. Live-coding languages

**TidalCycles** — Haskell-embedded DSL; patterns are functions of cycles, declared `d1 $ s "bd ~ bd ~"`, combined with `#`/`stack`/euclid `(3,8)` ([Tidal patterns reference](https://tidalcycles.org/docs/reference/patterns/)). Its MIDI layer is unusually complete for a live-coding language: `midibend :: Pattern Double -> ControlPattern` with the raw 14-bit range `0–16383` centred at `8192`, `miditouch` (0–127) for aftertouch, and `ccn`/`ccv` pairs, plus `nrpnn`/`nrpnv` ([Tidal MIDI docs](https://tidalcycles.org/docs/configuration/MIDIOSC/midi/)).
*(a)* Expression is a **parallel pattern, not a note attribute**: `d1 $ stack [midibend (segment 128 $ range 8193 16383 $ saw), note "c"] # midichan 4`. You choose the sample rate by hand (`segment 128`), you write raw 14-bit integers, and the bend lands on a *channel*, so it hits every note on that channel — the opposite of per-note. No MPE channel rotation is documented. *(b)* Chords/voicings are not in the core reference; users hand-roll `toScale`/note stacks. *(c)* Extremely fast for rhythm/timbre, slow for anything voiced. *(d)* MIDI out via SuperDirt with documented latency-calibration caveats.

**Strudel** — JavaScript port of Tidal, browser-native, method-chained: `s("bd,[~ <sd!3 sd(3,4,2)>],hh*8").speed(perlin.range(.7,.9))` ([Strudel getting started](https://strudel.cc/learn/getting-started/)). Continuous signals are first-class (`sine`, `saw`, `tri`, `perlin`, `rand`, `mouseX`, plus `-1..1` variants `sine2`) ([Strudel signals](https://strudel.cc/learn/signals/)). It has the best harmony story of any live-coding language: `voicing()` with `dict('ireal')`, `anchor`, and `mode` values `below/duck/above/root`, plus `voicings(range?)` documented as using "the smoothest voice leading possible" ([Strudel tonal API](https://strudel.cc/learn/tonal/), [Strudel docs mirror](https://patterns.slab.org/learn/tonal/), [understanding voicings](https://strudel.cc/understand/voicings/)).
*(a)* `midibend` is normalized `-1..1` and `miditouch` `0..1`; CC via `.ccn(74).ccv(sine.slow(4))` or declarative `midimaps({mymap:{lpf:{ccn:74,min:0,max:20000,exp:0.5}}})` ([Strudel MIDI I/O](https://strudel.cc/learn/input-output/)). Still channel-scoped: `midichan` defaults to 1 and is a single number per pattern; nothing allocates a channel per sounding note, so a chord cannot carry four independent bends. Alignment quirk documented: "structure comes from the left", so `ccv` inside a note pattern silently inherits note structure. *(b)* Best-in-class concision: `chord("<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2").dict('ireal').voicing()`. *(c)* Very fast. *(d)* WebMIDI; no MPE.

**Sonic Pi** — Ruby-flavoured imperative: `play 50, amp: 0.3, release: 2`, `live_loop :foo do ... end`, `with_fx`. Its distinctive expression mechanism is **running-synth control with `_slide`**: every controllable opt has a slide twin (`cutoff_slide:`, `note_slide:`, `pan_slide:`), and slides are "sticky" until reset; ADSR opts cannot be changed once set ([Sonic Pi tutorial](https://sonic-pi.net/tutorial.html)). MIDI out is a flat function set: `midi_note_on`, `midi_cc`, `midi_pitch_bend`, `midi_clock_tick` ([MIDI Out tutorial](https://github.com/sonic-pi-net/sonic-pi/blob/dev/etc/doc/tutorial/11.2-MIDI-Out.md)).
*(a)* `_slide` is genuinely the nicest *internal* continuous-expression idiom in live coding — but it targets Sonic Pi's own synths, is one-target-at-a-time, needs a variable handle per voice, and has no MIDI/MPE analogue: `midi_pitch_bend` sends to *all* devices/channels by default. *(b)* `chord`, `scale`, rings — no voice-leading. *(c)* Fast, verbose. *(d)* MIDI yes, MPE no.

**FoxDot** — Python; `p1 >> pads([0,1,2,3], dur=[1/4,3/4], sus=1, vib=4, scale=Scale.minor)`; player objects are two-char names, `>>` assigns a SynthDef ([FoxDot README](https://github.com/Qirky/FoxDot)). *(a)* Expression = SynthDef keyword args (`vib=4`) — a scalar per event, no curve, no shape, SuperCollider-only. *(b)* Scale degrees + `Scale`; no chord grammar. *(c)* Fast. *(d)* SuperCollider-first; MIDI is secondary.

**Gibber / gibberwocky.midi** — JS live coding, `Ctrl+Enter` per line, explicitly "live code MIDI messages for targeting synths and/or DAWs" ([gibberwocky.midi](https://github.com/gibber-cc/gibberwocky.midi)). Its DAW-targeting stance is the right instinct, but the repo documents no note/chord/expression syntax and no MPE.

**Mercury** — "minimal and human-readable" live-coding language; notes are a 2-D `note(interval octave)` system where `note(0 0)` = MIDI 36 and out-of-range intervals wrap and carry the octave: `set lead note(aMelody 1) time(1/8)` ([Mercury ICLC 2019 paper](https://iclc.toplap.org/2019/papers/paper67.pdf), [Mercury docs](https://tmhglnd.github.io/mercury/docs/)). *(a)/(b)* No documented expression curves, chord symbols, or voice leading; MIDI/OSC exist but are undocumented in the reference. Its design goal — "quick expression" via terse, reusable interval lists — is the ergonomics bar to beat, not the feature bar.

**Alda** — score-oriented text language: `piano: o4 c8 d e f+ g`, `<`/`>` octave, `+`/`-` accidentals, `~` ties, `|` cosmetic barlines, `V1:`/`V2:` voices, markers `%name`/`@name`, global attributes `(tempo! 200)` ([Alda tutorial](https://alda.io/tutorial/)). Expression is the attribute set `volume`, `track-volume`, `panning`, `quantization` — `quant` being "the percentage of a note's full duration that is heard", 0–100, default 90 ([Alda attributes](https://alda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/attributes/), [cheat sheet](https://alda.io/cheat-sheet/)). *(a)* This is the honest state of the art for *readable* text scores: articulation = note-length percentage; no bend, vibrato, pressure, or curve. *(b)* Chords via `c/e/g`; no functional harmony. *(c)* Very fast to read/write. *(d)* MIDI (JVM synth) only.

**Ziffers** — numeric/roman-numeral notation for Sonic Pi (and ports in Sardine): pitches `-2 -1 0 1 2`, chords `0 024 2 246`, durations `w h q e s`, subdivision `[1 2 [3 4]]`, octaves `^ _`, **roman chords `i ii iii+4 …`, named chords `i^7 i^min i^dim i^maj7`, modal interchange `iiia ig ivf^7`**, repeats `[: … :4]`, transformations `(0 1 2)<i>(-2 1)`, L-system `rules:` rewriting, and list algebra `((1 2 3)+(0 9 13))-2` ([Ziffers README/cheatsheet](https://github.com/amiika/ziffers), [Sardine Ziffers docs](https://sardine.raphaelforment.fr/pattern_languages/ziffers/lists.html)). *(b)* The densest harmony *notation* in live coding — roman numerals + modal interchange in ~4 characters. *(a)* Nothing for continuous expression; arpeggio helpers (`G1232`) are the closest to articulation. *(c)* Fastest ideation of the set ("live code golfing"). *(d)* Inherits Sonic Pi's MIDI.

**Orca** — grid language, one letter per operator, base-36 values, `:` = `midi(channel octave note velocity length)`, `=` = OSC, lowercase ops fire on bang, uppercase every frame ([Orca README](https://github.com/hundredrabbits/Orca)). Brilliant for procedural sequencing; expression is limited to velocity/length fields in a 36-step space. No harmony, no curves.

**Glicol** — Rust graph-oriented DSP language: `o: sin 440 >> mul ~amp` / `~amp: sin 1.0 >> mul 0.3 >> add 0.5`, sequencing via `seq 60 _~a _ 48__67` ([Glicol README](https://github.com/chaosprint/glicol/blob/main/README.md)). Continuous modulation is native *as audio-rate signal graph* — the cleanest text syntax for "a curve" anywhere in this survey (`~ref` chains) — but it is synthesis, not note-level MIDI; no chords, no MIDI/MPE out.

## 2. Notation / score languages

**LilyPond** — `\relative`-style declarative notation with a large symbolic articulation vocabulary (`\staccato`, slurs, trills, hairpins) under "Expressive marks" ([LilyPond notation reference](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/notation/expressive-marks)). Its MIDI story exposes the core problem of notation languages: expression is *symbolic*, and playback is approximated by **note-length scaling** via `articulate.ly` — "a 'staccato' halves the note value", "'tenuto' gives a note its full duration", and only for notes "not under slurs" ([MIDI articulation](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.25/Documentation/usage/midi-articulation)). Documented side effects: the script "may shorten chords", may shorten unmarked notes, and alters the engraved output to match MIDI; the manual recommends restricting `\articulate` to short segments ([Enhancing MIDI output](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.25/Documentation/notation/enhancing-midi-output)). *(a)* No bend/vibrato/pressure/CC curves at all. *(b)* Chords and figured bass, but no generative harmony. *(c)* Slow (engraving-oriented). *(d)* Basic MIDI.

**ABC notation** — one-letter-per-note ASCII: `^F`/`_F`/`=F` accidentals, `C,`/`c'` octaves, `L:1/8` unit length, inline fields `[M:9/8]`, decorations and chord symbols in the tune body ([ABC standard 2.1](https://abcnotation.com/wiki/abc:standard:v2.1)). Superb typing ergonomics; expression is a closed set of decoration glyphs; no continuous data, no MPE.

**MusicXML 4.0** — interchange XML. Harmony is genuinely well-modelled: `<harmony>` holds stacked "harmony-chords" with `<root>` *or* `<numeral>` (scale degree, Nashville) *or* deprecated `<function>`, plus `<kind>`, `<inversion>`, `<bass>`, `<degree>` — "V of II would be represented by a harmony-chord with a 5 numeral followed by a harmony-chord with a 2 numeral" ([`<harmony>` reference](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/harmony/)). Continuous expression exists only as guitar `<bend>`: `<bend-alter>`, `<pre-bend>`/`<release>`, `with-bar`, and playback hints `accelerate="no"`, `beats` = "the number of discrete elements, like MIDI pitch bends, used to represent a continuous bend", default `4`, with `first-beat=25` / `last-beat=75` ([`<bend>` reference](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/bend/)). That is the *entire* continuous-expression surface of the dominant interchange format — a 4-step bend with two percentage anchors, guitar-only. Documented translation pain: articulations are ambiguous between glyph and performance definition, and Finale/MusicXML round-trips prefer the font definition ([MusicXML translation issues](https://www.musicxml.com/publications/makemusic-recordare/max-2002/musicxml-translation-issues/)); the format is also explicitly aimed at Western staff notation ([MusicXML limitations survey](http://www.music.mcgill.ca/~ich/classes/mumt621_22/Assignments/Miranda%20Jackson/Jackson22MusicXML.pdf)).

**MNX** (W3C draft, JSON successor to MusicXML) — states it encodes "the notes, rhythms, pitches, parts", favours "one-and-only-one way to express concepts", and explicitly positions MIDI as "low-level instrument sequencing, as opposed to higher-level musical concepts" ([MNX docs](https://w3c.github.io/mnx/docs/)). No expression curves, no harmony/voice-leading, no MIDI/MPE mapping — i.e. the next-generation notation standard is *deliberately* not solving performance expression.

**GUIDO** — compact ASCII notation with sticky octaves/durations: `[c1 c c2 c c3 c]`, `[c/1 c d/2 d e/4 e]`, commands `\slur( d e f )`, `\meter<"4/4">`, staves `{ [...], [...] }` ([GUIDO overview](https://wiki.ccarh.org/wiki/Guido_Music_Notation)). Elegant stickiness model (a real ergonomic lesson); no continuous expression.

**Bol Processor** — generative grammars (BP1/BP2) over symbolic strings, with metavariables, remote contexts, weighted/probabilistic rules, subgrammars, homomorphisms ([BP grammars](https://bolprocessor.org/bol-processor-grammars/), [modelling music with grammars](https://bolprocessor.org/modelling-music-with-grammars/)). Unmatched for structural/rhythmic transformation; documentation shows no harmony vocabulary and no continuous per-note control.

**SuperCollider patterns** — `Pbind(\degree, …, \dur, …)` builds Events (dictionaries) whose keys map to SynthDef controls (`\freq`, `\amp`, `\pan`, `\gate`), read by an `EventStreamPlayer`; `'delta'` sets time to next event ([Pattern Guide 03](https://depts.washington.edu/dxscdoc/Help/Tutorials/A-Practical-Guide/PG_03_What_Is_Pbind.html)). The event-as-open-dictionary model is the most extensible substrate here — any expression key you invent can ride along — but expression is one *scalar per event*, curves must be pushed into the SynthDef, and the syntax is verbose for fast ideation.

**Csound score** — positional p-fields; `f` statements build function tables: `f 1 0 1024 10 1`, with `p3` size, `p4` GEN number, negative `p4` disabling rescaling ([`f` statement](https://csound.com/manual/scoregens/f/)); score-level time warping via `v` and `t` ([score statements](https://csound.com/manual/reference/scoreStatementsRef/)). F-tables are the classic text encoding of a *shape*, and per-note expression is possible by passing breakpoints as extra p-fields into `linseg`/`transeg` — but the shape is anonymous, untyped, positionally addressed, and utterly unreadable at a glance; there is no note↔curve binding, no harmony, no MIDI out.

## 4. Expressive MIDI / MPE tooling — the transport is ready, the notation is missing

- **MPE 1.1** (MMA/AMEI Recommended Practice, replacing RP-053) defines per-note control by assigning "individual MIDI Channels", explicitly avoiding Mono Mode's polyphony cap ([MIDI.org MPE](https://midi.org/mpe-midi-polyphonic-expression)). Operationally: channel rotation, one channel per sounding note, global/Master Channel per Zone (Ch 1 lower / Ch 16 upper), notes on channels 2–16, **per-note bend default ±48 semitones vs ±2 on Master, settable 0–±96 via RPN 0**, timbre on **CC74**, loudness on Channel Pressure ([StudioCode MPE reference](https://studiocode.dev/resources/mpe/), [Polyphonic Expression: how it works](https://polyphonicexpression.com/mpe-how-it-works)). Controller dimension names differ per vendor (Roli Strike/Glide/Slide/Press/Lift vs LinnStrument X/Y/Z) ([moForte MPE table](https://www.moforte.com/mpe/)).
- **Data-rate reality**: a four-note chord with four independent vibratos at 20 msg/s/note = **80 pitch-bend messages per second**, vs 20 for one global bend ([how MPE works](https://polyphonicexpression.com/mpe-how-it-works)). Any generator emitting text-authored curves must budget message density.
- **Logic Pro** implements MPE through per-instrument **MIDI Mono mode**, `On (with common base channel 1)`, a `Mono Mode Pitch Range` field, and **polyphony limited to 15 notes**; only some instruments qualify (Alchemy, ES2, Sculpture, Sampler…) ([Apple: use MPE with software instruments](https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro/use-mpe-with-software-instruments-lgcp8f599497/mac)).
- **Bitwig** exposes velocity/timbre/pressure as per-note expression lanes editable by hand-drawing (e.g. a timbre curve "from 60% down to 11%" for one note) ([Expressions guide](https://polarity.me/posts/bitwig-guides/2022-05-31-expressions-bitwig-modulator-guide/)). Editing MPE across DAWs remains a known friction point; multi-channel per-note editing is a recurring complaint ([Reaper feature thread](https://forum.cockos.com/showthread.php?t=177133), [KVR MPE support discussion](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=587412&start=15)).
- **MIDI 2.0** adds Per-Note Controllers, Per-Note Pitch Bend, 16-bit velocity (0–65,535), 32-bit CC/pressure/pitch (~4.2 billion steps), Note On Attribute Type/Data, and Registered Controllers for pedal gestures (hammer/damper position, half-pedaling, sostenuto, una corda) — "much finer pitch curves; better vibrato/bends and editing" ([MIDI.org: state of MIDI 2.0, Feb 2026](https://midi.org/the-state-of-midi-2-0-high-resolution-performance-and-the-rise-of-profiles-update-feb-2026)); an MPE Profile exists for MIDI-CI ([MPE Profile v2 PDF](https://amei-music.github.io/midi2.0-docs/amei-pdf/M2-120-UM_v2-0-3_MIDI_Polyphonic_Expression_Profile.pdf)).
- **Expression maps** (Cubase/Dorico) are the sample-library-facing counterpart: articulations are entered symbolically and mapped to key switches / program change / CC, with the closest match used if none fits ([Cubase expression maps](https://www.steinberg.help/r/cubase-pro/14.0/en/cubase_nuendo/topics/expression_maps/expression_maps_c.html)). Dorico adds base vs add-on switches, a pitch-bend range field in half-steps, microtonality method selection, and **note-length conditions** with values *Very short* (0.1875 s) / *Short* (0.375 s) / *Medium* (0.75 s) / *Long* (1.5 s) / *Very long* and operators `== != < <= > >=` ([Dorico Expression Maps dialog](https://www.steinberg.help/r/dorico-pro/6.1/en/dorico/topics/library/library_expression_maps_dialog_r.html)). This is a **declarative rule language for articulation dispatch** — but it lives in a GUI dialog, is per-library, and only routes discrete techniques.
- **SFZ** is the richest existing *text* vocabulary for continuous expression, but on the **response** side: `lobend`/`hibend` (−8192..8192), `bend_up`/`bend_down` in cents (−9600..9600), `bend_smooth` ("adds inertia"), `bend_step`/`bend_stepup`/`bend_stepdown`, and pitch LFO opcodes `pitchlfo_delay`, `pitchlfo_depth` (±1200 cents), `pitchlfo_depthccN`, `pitchlfo_depthchanaft`, `pitchlfo_depthpolyaft`, `pitchlfo_fade` ([SFZ opcodes](https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/)). It encodes *how an instrument reacts*, never *what the performer does on beat 3*.
- **ASCII tab** is the only mass-adopted text notation for bends — and it is explicitly inadequate: "accounting for bends and vibrato in a text-based tab sheet is tricky… there's no way to accurately notate" them, leaving just `b` ([Guitar Pro on bends and vibrato](https://www.guitar-pro.com/blog/p/12520-bends-and-vibrato-in-guitar-pro-6)); the GUI model instead has typed bend shapes (Bend, Bend/Release, Prebend, Prebend/Release, Bend+Vibrato) with extents of ½/1/1½/2 steps. That typed-shape-with-extent vocabulary is exactly what no text language has.

## 6. Synthesis: where the genuine white space is

**Already solved — do not rebuild.** Chord suggestion and corpus-driven reharmonization (Hookpad's Magic Chord, Scaler's Colors/modulation pathways, RapidComposer's rules). Scale quantization and key filtering (Bitwig Key Filter, Strudel `scale()`). Arpeggiation, strumming, humanize knobs (Cthulhu, InstaChord, Captain's Space/Strum/Swing). Euclidean rhythm and cycle-based pattern algebra (Tidal/Strudel mini-notation is a solved, excellent design — adopt, don't reinvent). Concise voicing with smooth voice leading *as a function* (Strudel `voicings()`, `dict('ireal')`). Roman-numeral/modal-interchange notation (Ziffers, MusicXML `<numeral>`). Engraving (LilyPond). Articulation dispatch to sample libraries (expression maps). Generic "AI writes MIDI" (Orb/LANDR, Captain).

**The five real gaps.**

1. **Expression is never a property of a note.** In every text system surveyed, continuous expression is either a *parallel channel-scoped stream* (Tidal/Strudel `midibend`, `ccv`), a *scalar event field* (SC patterns, FoxDot, Alda `quant`), an *instrument-side response rule* (SFZ, C15 macros), or a *symbolic glyph* (LilyPond, ABC, MusicXML). Nothing lets you write a note and bind a *named, parameterized, time-normalized curve* to that note's own lifetime, such that transposing, revoicing, or reharmonizing carries the gesture with it. MPE 1.1 and MIDI 2.0 provide the wire format (per-note channels, ±48 semitones, CC74, 32-bit per-note controllers) that would make such bindings audible today; the authoring notation simply does not exist. Bitwig proves the *editing* model works — but only with a mouse, per note, undiffable.

2. **No system is harmony-aware in its expression, or expression-aware in its harmony.** Scaler/RapidComposer/Hookpad know functional harmony and emit inert MIDI; MPE tools carry rich per-note data with no harmonic model. Nobody can express "vibrato only on non-chord tones", "bend the guide tone into the next chord's third", "pressure swell peaks at the dominant resolution", or "top voice leads the ensemble by 18 ms, bass trails". Both halves of the vocabulary exist; the *join* is empty. This is the single most defensible claim for a new language.

3. **Voices are anonymous.** Strudel's `voicings()` produces smooth voice leading but the resulting notes have no identity, so per-voice expressive differentiation is impossible. Divisi exists in Scaler and voices exist in Alda (`V1:`/`V2:`) and MusicXML (`<voice>`), but never joined to per-note expression. A language with **named, persistent voices that carry both a voice-leading constraint and an expression profile** (soprano vibrato onset later and shallower than alto; bass no bend) is unclaimed territory.

4. **No render-target abstraction for expression.** Dorico/Cubase expression maps show the right shape — declarative rules, note-length conditions with real operators, base vs add-on switches — but they are GUI dialogs bound to specific libraries and only route *discrete* techniques. Nothing lets one text score render as (i) MPE with channel rotation and RPN-set bend range, (ii) CC/keyswitch for an orchestral library, (iii) MIDI 2.0 per-note controllers, from the same gesture description. A text-authored, versionable analogue of expression maps — SFZ-like precision on the target side, gesture-typed on the source side — is genuine white space and is also the credible interop story.

5. **Continuous expression is not diffable, reusable, or parameterizable anywhere.** ASCII tab admits defeat on bends; Csound f-tables are anonymous positional numbers; DAW curves are mouse geometry. A typed gesture library — bend shapes with extent and acceleration (Guitar Pro's taxonomy: bend, bend/release, prebend, prebend/release, bend+vibrato; MusicXML's `accelerate`/`first-beat`/`last-beat`), vibrato with delay/rate ramp/depth envelope (SFZ's `pitchlfo_delay`/`fade`/`depth` proves the parameter set), pressure envelopes, per-voice micro-timing offsets — expressed as short composable tokens with sensible instrument-idiom defaults, would be new, and would be testable/versionable in a way no GUI is.

**The quality bar to clear.** Terseness: Ziffers (a roman-numeral progression with modal interchange in ~10 characters) and Hookpad (one digit per chord). Readability: Alda and GUIDO's sticky-state model. Composability: Tidal/Strudel pattern algebra and SuperCollider's open event dictionary. Precision on the target side: SFZ opcode granularity and Dorico's condition operators. Numeric correctness: MPE's ±48 semitone per-note bend, CC74 timbre, channel pressure, Zone/Master channel split, 15-note practical polyphony (Logic Mono mode), and a message-density budget (~20 msg/s/note).

**Sharpest one-line thesis.** The white space is not "another live-coding language" and not "another chord generator": it is a text notation where *per-note expressive gestures are typed, named, harmony-aware first-class values attached to identified voices, and compiled to MPE / MIDI 2.0 / CC-keyswitch targets through declarative maps.* Every ingredient exists in isolation; nothing composes them.
